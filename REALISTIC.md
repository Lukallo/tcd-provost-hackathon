# Realistic ideas: small, profitable, startable this term

Criteria: **under ~€1,000 to start**, customers you can reach yourself, simple revenue, no regulations or carbon markets needed,
and a test you could run **within weeks**. All € figures are rough guesses; check them with real prices before the pitch.

## The three most realistic

### 1. Rent-a-real Christmas tree
- **Problem:** Cut Christmas trees are grown for years, used for a few weeks, then binned or chipped.
- **Business:** Rent potted living trees for December, deliver and collect, keep them in a field or nursery, rent them again
  next year. Customers pay a rental fee + deposit.
- **Why it's realistic:** The demand already exists, the timing is perfect (the challenge is in October, so you can pre-sell in
  November and run a real pilot in December 2026), and it's easy to understand.
- **Prototype on the day:** booking page mock-up + unit economics (tree cost, rental price, survival rate, storage).
- **Key risks:** do potted trees survive indoors (needs a care guide + short rental window)? Where are they stored?
  Are there competitors already in Dublin? (Check; similar services exist in the UK.)

### 2. Debs & ball dress rental
- **Problem:** Formal outfits are worn once. Debs, balls and weddings mean lots of new purchases every year.
- **Business:** Build a stock from second-hand / donated outfits, rent per event, with dry-cleaning included.
  Run pop-up fitting days at schools or at Trinity before ball season.
- **Why it's realistic:** Small upfront stock, clear seasonal demand, and the payback per item is likely after one or two rentals.
- **Prototype on the day:** an Instagram page mock-up + one rail of outfits (borrowed) + price list.
- **Key risks:** damage/stains (deposit), sizing range, how much people care about wearing something new for the photos.

### 3. "We sell it for you"
- **Problem:** Everyone has stuff that's worth money but won't spend the time listing it, so it gets binned or
  sits in a cupboard.
- **Business:** Pick up, photograph, list, ship; keep a commission. Unsold items go to charity shops.
- **Why it's realistic:** Zero stock, zero capital, revenue from week one, and it's easy to show the impact (items kept from landfill).
- **Prototype on the day:** take 10 real items (bring them!), list them live during the hackathon, and show the
  views/offers in the pitch.
- **Key risks:** margin on cheap items (set a minimum value), time per item.

## How to make any of these win the judging
- **Impact:** count it: items reused, kg kept out of the bin, € saved per customer.
- **Feasibility:** you can say "we can start next week", which beats most pitches in the room.
- **Human-centred:** talk to 5 likely customers before 24 Oct (parents of debs students; people who bought a Christmas tree).
- **Innovation:** this is the weak spot for simple businesses. Add one twist, e.g. trees with a tracker so you can see "your" tree growing
  in the field during the year, or dress rental where every outfit has a QR code with its rental history.
