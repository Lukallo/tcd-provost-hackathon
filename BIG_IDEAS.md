# Big, innovative ideas (checked against what already exists)

Every idea here was searched against existing companies and research (9 Oct 2026). The **gap** is what we could not find
anyone doing commercially. "Couldn't find" isn't proof nobody does it, so do a deeper check before pitching.

## Ideas that survived the check

### 1. Gorse → peat-free mushroom casing ⭐
- **Two problems, one material.** Gorse is the main fuel in Ireland's upland fires. Separately, Ireland's mushroom industry
  needs peat for its "casing" layer, domestic peat harvesting has stopped, and growers have been importing peat from Latvia.
  Teagasc's *Beyond Peat* project (2021–) says no alternative fully matches peat yet.
- **Evidence it could work:** University of Santiago de Compostela trials found gorse compost worked as a peat substitute for
  strawberries and mostly met EU eco-label requirements.
- **Gap:** no one is using gorse for **mushroom casing**, and no Irish gorse-to-growing-media business turned up.
- **Business:** paid to clear gorse (fire-risk reduction for NPWS, councils, farmers) **+** sell casing to mushroom growers.
  Fewer fires, no more peat imports, bogs left intact, rural jobs.
- **Biggest risks (make these your "key assumptions"):** gorse compost holds *little plant-available water*, and casing needs
  a lot of it, so it probably needs blending (e.g. with bark, which Teagasc is testing). Weed seeds (control with
  composting heat). Harvesting cost on slopes (a Spanish shrub harvester ran ~€54 per dry tonne).
- **Prototype:** business model + a map of upland fire areas next to mushroom-farm clusters + a casing blend spec.
  Interview: a Teagasc *Beyond Peat* researcher and one mushroom grower.

### 2. Autonomous bog sluice
- **Idea:** solar-powered automatic weirs on drains in drained peat. They raise the water table *before* a forecast dry
  spell (stopping fire and carbon loss) and lower it when farmers need access for grazing.
- **Exists:** the pieces exist separately: Finnish remote-controlled drainage wells with model-predictive control, Swiss
  retrofit weirs, Cambridge's *PeatWaterWise* water-table monitoring.
- **Gap:** couldn't find a deployed system that combines **automatic weirs + peat + drought forecast**.
- **Business:** hardware + subscription to landowners, peat restoration programmes and NPWS. Pairs with Smoulder sensors.

### 3. Rural broadband fibre as a fire sensor
- **Idea:** fibre-optic sensing reads heat and vibration along a cable. Use the fibre that Ireland's National Broadband Plan ran
  on poles through rural and upland areas as a fire sensor covering thousands of km, with no new hardware in the field.
- **Exists:** Prisma Photonics does this on **power-line** fibre (the earth wire on top of transmission lines); there was a field
  demonstration on a brushfire in Israel in 2023.
- **Gap:** **telecom/broadband fibre** used for wildfire detection; couldn't find it.
- **Business:** revenue share with the network owner; sell alerts to fire services, ESB and insurers.
- **Risks:** fibre has to be close to the fire; whether aerial cable is sensitive enough; network owner's cooperation.

### 4. Biodiversity census from air-quality filters
- **Idea:** air-quality monitoring stations already suck air through filters, and those filters capture DNA from plants and animals.
  Sequence them to get a nationwide biodiversity survey from existing infrastructure, including **old filter archives**,
  so you get decades of past data.
- **Exists:** research only: the UK National Physical Laboratory's air monitor; a Swedish station archive found 2,700+ taxa and showed a
  decline. NatureMetrics is building commercial air sampling with **its own devices**.
- **Gap:** a commercial service running on **existing national air-quality networks** (e.g. the EPA's in Ireland).
- **Business:** governments need biodiversity monitoring for EU nature reporting; companies need it for nature disclosures.
- **Risks:** false positives; whether the data shows real trends over time; access to the filters.

### 5. Seaweed-based fire retardant
- **Idea:** a biodegradable fire retardant made from Irish seaweed (alginate) to replace phosphate retardants such as Phos-Chek,
  which are criticised for trace metals and algal blooms in rivers. The manufacturer disputes this.
- **Exists:** the British Forestry Commission tested sodium alginate as a retardant in 1958–61. AlgoBio (Waterloo) makes seaweed-based
  fire-retardant *coatings*. Berkeley's Greener Solutions studied plant-based char-formers.
- **Gap:** couldn't find a commercial alginate-based long-term retardant for wildfires.
- **Business:** sell to firefighting agencies. Ireland has a strong seaweed industry to supply it.
- **Risks:** lots of science; certification takes years; cost per litre against phosphate.

## Round 2: beyond environmental monitoring (checked 9 Oct 2026)

### 7. Over-the-shop homes in a box ⭐ (Sustainable Communities): GAP
- **Idea:** a turnkey service that converts vacant upper floors in Irish towns into homes using prefab pods that fit through narrow
  stairs and are designed for fire rules in advance, **with the grant paperwork done for the owner**.
- **Why now:** above-the-shop grants pay up to €95k per unit (€135k in Limerick). DCU, University of Galway and Dublin Simon published research
  on these units in 2024. A Cork councillor said owners lack the "know-how to tie it all together".
- **Exists:** UK pods that come in flat panels (Off Site Solutions); temporary modules inside vacant buildings (Studio Bark / Lowe Guardians, 2017).
- **Gap:** no Irish firm combines pods, fire compliance and grants into one product. **Real barriers: fire regulations and access.**
- **Who pays:** building owners, paid for by the grant. You take a fee per conversion.

### 8. Wild venison for school and hospital meals (Responsible Consumption / Nature): Irish gap
- **Idea:** Ireland culls deer to protect forests, but almost none of the meat reaches Irish plates. Build the supply chain
  (cull → processing → public procurement) for school meals, hospitals and prisons.
- **Proof it works:** Argyll & Bute Council and Wild Jura (Scotland) serve culled venison in school meals; it won the UK Public
  Sector Catering Award 2025.
- **Gap:** no Irish public-procurement route. Ireland's deer strategy group focuses on exports. Processing capacity is tiny
  (185 deer went to meat in 2018).
- **Who pays:** public catering budgets; local, lean, low-carbon protein.

### 9. Heat the person, not the house (People & Wellbeing / Climate): Irish gap
- **Idea:** heated vests and blankets for fuel-poor older people. Warming one person costs a fraction of heating a home.
- **Exists:** about 900 heated over-blankets given out by Cambridge Ahead and Allia (UK, 2022–23); Swansea University's study of a heated seat cover.
- **Gap:** no Irish scheme, and no trial of heated *wearables* measuring health, comfort and the € and CO₂ saved.
- **Who pays:** energy suppliers' energy-poverty obligations, charities (ALONE, SVP), the HSE.

### 10. Peat-free mushroom casing from Irish bogs (Responsible Consumption): partly exists
- **Idea:** link **sphagnum moss grown on rewetted Irish bogs** to Irish mushroom growers who need casing (combine with #1
  gorse as the bulk material).
- **Exists:** Palus Demos (EU-funded) grows sphagnum on Ballaghurt Bog, Offaly, but for general compost.
  BIOSCHAMP (NL) found a 50% sphagnum / 50% peat casing worked on 4 EU mushroom farms.
- **Gap:** nobody connects Irish-grown sphagnum to Irish mushroom casing or tests peat-free blends under Irish conditions.

### 11. Dairy milk cooling as grid flexibility (Climate): partly exists
- **Idea:** pool the milk-cooling load across many dairy farms and run the coolers when wind power is surplus; get paid
  by the grid for the flexibility.
- **Exists:** Teagasc/MTU ice builder that runs on cheap night tariffs; Yield Energy (US) does this with farm pumps.
- **Gap:** nobody pools Irish dairy cooling for grid flexibility payments.
- **Caveats:** ice banks use ~32% more energy per litre than cooling milk directly; Irish milk volume drops sharply in winter.

### 12. Sensory-friendly routes (People & Wellbeing): partly exists
- **Idea:** walking navigation built for neurodivergent people that plans the route around crowds, harsh lighting and sudden noise,
  not just traffic noise.
- **Exists:** Go Jauntly / Tranquil City "Green Routes" (UK); OpenRouteService quiet routing (DE); HushMap (rates venues, not routes).
- **Gap:** routing built for neurodivergent users; nothing for Ireland.

### 13. Smart-meter check-ins for older people (People & Wellbeing): exists abroad
- CENSIS / University of Edinburgh trial (Scotland), TEPCO + Informetis (Japan). **Irish gap:** use ESB's existing smart-meter
  data with no new hardware. Privacy and false alarms are the big risks.

### 14. Typha insulation from rewetted bogs (Climate): partly exists
- Typha Technik (DE) makes insulation boards from typha (bulrush), but couldn't scale because not enough typha is grown. **Irish gap:** grow typha on rewetted bogs and
  supply it to an insulation maker.

### 15. Spent mushroom compost → biochar / growing media (Responsible Consumption): partly exists
- BIOrescue (Monaghan biorefinery, EU-funded), Teagasc casing trials. **Gap:** no Irish commercial product. Every farm's
  compost is different, which is the hurdle.

### Dropped in round 2
| Idea | Why |
|---|---|
| Oyster reefs on offshore wind foundations | The Rich North Sea (NL), Codling Wind Park + UCD (IE) already doing it |
| Bog cotton textiles | Fibres too short to spin into yarn |
| Wool as mushroom casing | Untested, probably for a reason: wool's nitrogen helps competing moulds grow |
| Biodegradable bale wrap | Netting exists (Nature's Net Wrap); airtight film is a materials-science problem |
