# Big, innovative ideas (checked against what already exists)

Every idea here was searched against existing companies and research (9 Oct 2026). The **gap** is what we could not find
anyone doing commercially. "Couldn't find" isn't proof nobody does it, so do a deeper check before pitching.

## Ideas that survived the check

### 1. Gorse → peat-free mushroom casing ⭐
- **Two problems, one material.** Gorse is the main fuel in Ireland's upland fires. Separately, Ireland's mushroom industry
  needs peat for its "casing" layer, domestic peat harvesting has stopped, and growers have been importing peat from Latvia.
  Teagasc's *Beyond Peat* project (2021–) says no alternative fully matches peat yet.
- **Evidence it could work:** University of Santiago de Compostela trials found gorse compost worked as a peat substitute for
  strawberries and mostly met EU eco-label requirements.
- **Gap:** no one is using gorse for **mushroom casing**, and no Irish gorse-to-growing-media business turned up.
- **Business:** paid to clear gorse (fire-risk reduction for NPWS, councils, farmers) **+** sell casing to mushroom growers.
  Fewer fires, no more peat imports, bogs left intact, rural jobs.
- **Biggest risks (make these your "key assumptions"):** gorse compost holds *little plant-available water*, and casing needs
  a lot of it, so it probably needs blending (e.g. with bark, which Teagasc is testing). Weed seeds (control with
  composting heat). Harvesting cost on slopes (a Spanish shrub harvester ran ~€54 per dry tonne).
- **Prototype:** business model + a map of upland fire areas next to mushroom-farm clusters + a casing blend spec.
  Interview: a Teagasc *Beyond Peat* researcher and one mushroom grower.

### 2. Autonomous bog sluice
- **Idea:** solar-powered automatic weirs on drains in drained peat. They raise the water table *before* a forecast dry
  spell (stopping fire and carbon loss) and lower it when farmers need access for grazing.
- **Exists:** the pieces exist separately: Finnish remote-controlled drainage wells with model-predictive control, Swiss
  retrofit weirs, Cambridge's *PeatWaterWise* water-table monitoring.
- **Gap:** couldn't find a deployed system that combines **automatic weirs + peat + drought forecast**.
- **Business:** hardware + subscription to landowners, peat restoration programmes and NPWS. Pairs with Smoulder sensors.

### 3. Rural broadband fibre as a fire sensor
- **Idea:** fibre-optic sensing reads heat and vibration along a cable. Use the fibre that Ireland's National Broadband Plan ran
  on poles through rural and upland areas as a fire sensor covering thousands of km, with no new hardware in the field.
- **Exists:** Prisma Photonics does this on **power-line** fibre (the earth wire on top of transmission lines); there was a field
  demonstration on a brushfire in Israel in 2023.
- **Gap:** **telecom/broadband fibre** used for wildfire detection; couldn't find it.
- **Business:** revenue share with the network owner; sell alerts to fire services, ESB and insurers.
- **Risks:** fibre has to be close to the fire; whether aerial cable is sensitive enough; network owner's cooperation.

### 4. Biodiversity census from air-quality filters
- **Idea:** air-quality monitoring stations already suck air through filters, and those filters capture DNA from plants and animals.
  Sequence them to get a nationwide biodiversity survey from existing infrastructure, including **old filter archives**,
  so you get decades of past data.
- **Exists:** research only: the UK National Physical Laboratory's air monitor; a Swedish station archive found 2,700+ taxa and showed a
  decline. NatureMetrics is building commercial air sampling with **its own devices**.
- **Gap:** a commercial service running on **existing national air-quality networks** (e.g. the EPA's in Ireland).
- **Business:** governments need biodiversity monitoring for EU nature reporting; companies need it for nature disclosures.
- **Risks:** false positives; whether the data shows real trends over time; access to the filters.

### 5. Seaweed-based fire retardant
- **Idea:** a biodegradable fire retardant made from Irish seaweed (alginate) to replace phosphate retardants such as Phos-Chek,
  which are criticised for trace metals and algal blooms in rivers. The manufacturer disputes this.
- **Exists:** the British Forestry Commission tested sodium alginate as a retardant in 1958–61. AlgoBio (Waterloo) makes seaweed-based
  fire-retardant *coatings*. Berkeley's Greener Solutions studied plant-based char-formers.
- **Gap:** couldn't find a commercial alginate-based long-term retardant for wildfires.
- **Business:** sell to firefighting agencies. Ireland has a strong seaweed industry to supply it.
- **Risks:** lots of science; certification takes years; cost per litre against phosphate.
